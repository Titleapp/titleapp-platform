## 1. Executive Summary

- **The product stack already has four real layers, not two** — Worker, Bundle (Business-in-a-Box or Flagship App), and Creator Storefront — but only the first three are things SOCIII fully owns and operates end to end. The fourth is a genuinely different kind of thing: a product surface controlled by an external party. Conflating it with the others is the actual source of the "cat herding" risk, not creator volume by itself.
- **The technical mechanism a creator-publishing model would need already exists and is already in production.** `apps/business/src/main.jsx` reads a build-time `VITE_NATIVE_FLAVOR` env var (plus per-flavor overrides like `VITE_NATIVE_RE_COMPANY`/`VITE_NATIVE_RE_PERSONA`) to make one shared Capacitor project (`apps/business/capacitor.config.json`, appId `ai.sociii.app`, `webDir: dist`) boot straight into a specific worker on native launch — this is how the aviation native app and at least one real-estate tenant flavor (Merritt Capital) already work today. A creator-scoped native wrapper is the same mechanism with a new flavor value, not new engineering.
- **The real reason to keep creator storefronts off native app stores is economic, not just operational.** Apple/Google take 15–30% off any revenue that flows through their in-app purchase rails. That comes out of the pool before SOCIII's own revenue share applies — so every creator subscriber paying via IAP shrinks the split for both the creator and SOCIII. Web/Stripe checkout (already the platform's payment rail) avoids this entirely.
- **The capability contract registry (`contracts/capabilities.json`) is real, production infrastructure — but the pipeline that would let creator publishing actually scale on top of it does not exist yet, and the first draft of this doc overstated how far along that is.** The registry already enforces `allowedCallers`/`requiredKyc`/`requiredRoles`/`writesAudit` per capability today, for SOCIII's own hand-authored workers. Nothing today validates a creator-submitted ruleset against it at submission time — no intake pipeline, no Tier B scan, nothing (§2.5). Read "the mechanism exists" as: the enforcement primitive is proven; the thing that actually makes creator publishing scale without a human in the loop per submission is 100% unbuilt.
- **Capability scoping bounds what a worker can *do*, not what it can *say* — this distinction was blurred in the first draft and is corrected throughout this revision (§4).** A creator worker restricted to a harmless-looking `chat` capability can still dispense unsafe medical, legal, or financial advice in plain text; no elevated capability is required for that risk. Given SOCIII's own vertical roster skews regulated-adjacent (nursing, mortgage servicing, real estate), this is the single biggest residual risk in the creator-publishing model, not a footnote.
- **The liability boundary (#7) is Sean's stated business intent, not a legal resolution.** SOCIII runs the model, hosts auth, processes billing, takes a revenue cut, and round-trips entitlement through its own system for every creator-storefront session — that's active operational participation, which is exactly what plaintiffs' counsel and regulators look at, regardless of ToS language. Nothing in this doc constitutes a ToS clause, an indemnification agreement, or an insurance conversation, and SOCIII currently has no cyber/tech E&O coverage at all. Counsel review is a real prerequisite before this framework should be treated as settled, not just documented here.
- **This same session already produced one real precedent for "entitlements instead of a new app":** PETRA's Operations/Leasing/Compliance role-switcher (shipped 2026-09-17, `utils/propertyRole.js` + `PropertyRoleChooser.jsx` + Sidebar wiring) mirrors SKYE's aviation Pilot/MX/Dispatch pattern (`utils/crewRole.js`). Title search was seriously considered as a second real-estate app and rejected for exactly the "that's 2x iOS x Android for one buyer-type difference" reason driving this whole doc — it should become PETRA's fourth role instead, using the same entitlement mechanism, not a new build.
- **Mortgage servicing (Dana, `msr-servicing-001`) is real evidence the "one engine, vertical-specific compliance layer" pattern already works.** Its loan schema (`borrowerName`, UPB, `status`, `escrowAnnualTotal` — already optional/nullable) is largely asset-class-agnostic; what's mortgage-specific is the RAAS compliance layer (HUD-approved-housing-counselor referral language, RESPA framing baked into the borrower-persona chat guardrails at `index.js:6229-6240`). Extending Dana to auto or other securitized lending is mostly a new `raas/lending/{asset-class}/` vertical baseline, the same shape as `raas/real-estate/CA` vs `FL` vs `TX` already is — not new core engineering.

### 2.3 Business-in-a-Box — a real, distinct precedent, already shipping

Education Business in a Box (Ada persona, Course Uploader wizard) and Compliance in a Box (Elara/DPP) are real, active builds this quarter — curated operational bundles sold to a business running that specific business type, not branded consumer products. This is a genuinely different GTM motion from a Flagship App: the buyer is "give me the back-office stack to run an X business," not "I want the branded Y experience."

### 2.4 Flagship apps and the native-wrap decision rule — already deliberate, already selective

Native (Capacitor) wraps are not applied uniformly even within one flagship app. The existing, deliberate rule (tracked since 2026-09-05): native investment goes to consumer/field-facing use (on-the-go, phone-camera-driven, or literally can't be at a desk) — Pilot and MX in aviation, tenant/customer side in real estate, the DPP customer scan flow. Desk-based back-office work stays web-only regardless of vertical — aviation Dispatch, real-estate title-company/property-manager admin, nursing instructor side. **This is the same rule that resolved the title-search-as-separate-app question earlier today**: title-company back-office users are desk-based the same way Dispatch is, so folding title search into PETRA as an entitled role, web-only, matches a rule the platform already applies elsewhere — it isn't a new exception.

### 2.5 The capability contract registry as an enforcement mechanism, not just documentation

`contracts/capabilities.json` already declares, per capability: `allowedCallers`, `requiredKyc`, `requiredRoles`, `emitsEvent`, `writesAudit`. Per `CLAUDE.md`'s own stated invariant, "if a capability is not declared there, it does not exist" — this is a real, load-bearing enforcement point today, not aspirational language. The gap for creator publishing specifically: every entry today is hand-authored by a SOCIII engineer describing a SOCIII-built worker. Nothing today validates a creator-submitted worker/ruleset against this registry at submission time — that pipeline doesn't exist (Open Decision #4).

### 2.6 The native-wrapper mechanism a creator model would reuse

`apps/business/src/main.jsx` (lines ~10, 35–36, 107–109) already implements exactly the mechanism a creator-scoped app would need: a build-time `VITE_NATIVE_FLAVOR` value plus flavor-specific overrides, read at launch to set `ta_redirect_page` and send the user straight into one specific worker/persona, bypassing the general workspace hub entirely. One shared Capacitor project (`apps/business/capacitor.config.json`) already produces multiple differently-scoped native experiences from the same `dist` build this way. A "creator flavor" is the same pattern with a new value — real, low-risk, already-proven infrastructure, not a new subsystem.

## 4. The Creator-Publishing Boundary

This is the actual strategic decision this doc exists to record.

**The problem, precisely stated:** letting creators self-serve into their own native app multiplies four costs that don't scale linearly with creator count — a distinct App Store/Play Store developer relationship and review cycle per creator, an OS-compatibility maintenance treadmill per app, a support inbox per creator, and (per §4 below) a real revenue loss to store fees. None of these get cheaper by having more infrastructure; they get more expensive by having more creators.

**The proposed boundary:** a creator's worker is always reachable as a **layer-2 storefront** — SOCIII's shared marketplace, a creator-branded web page, checkout on SOCIII's existing Stripe rail. SOCIII never builds, publishes, or maintains a native app on a creator's behalf at this layer.

**If a creator specifically wants a native app anyway:** SOCIII hands them a **thin wrapper, not a fork** — a generic Capacitor shell (the same mechanism in §2.6, extended with a new `VITE_NATIVE_FLAVOR` value scoped to that creator's single worker). **Corrected by the red-team pass (was internally inconsistent with #6 as first drafted):** a SOCIII engineer hand-prepares that flavor-scoped repo/config per creator — that's the "stays manual" part of #6 — but the actual compile, code-signing, and store submission is always done by the creator, under **their own developer account, using their own tooling.** SOCIII never receives, holds, or uses a creator's Apple/Google signing credentials at any point; a platform holding third-party signing credentials is its own real security/liability exposure and this model deliberately avoids it. Critically, the wrapper still calls SOCIII's hosted API:
- The revenue-share relationship continues unchanged on that traffic (Sean, 2026-09-17: keep the split the same).
- The capability registry still governs everything the worker can do — publishing a native shell doesn't grant the creator's app any capability their worker wasn't already entitled to.
- **Authentication round-trips through SOCIII, confirmed by Sean this session** — a subscriber's entitlement lives in SOCIII's system, not the creator's. This is what keeps the hand-off a wrapper-and-continue-the-relationship model rather than a disguised full exit.
- What actually transfers to the creator is narrow and deliberate: the App Store/Play Store developer account, the review process, the ongoing OS-compatibility burden, and the 15–30% store cut on whatever revenue flows through that specific native channel. The underlying platform IP — the RAAS engine, the rules for other tenants, other workers — never leaves SOCIII's infrastructure, consistent with `CLAUDE.md`'s own framing of defensive IP as the engine and record model, not the UI.

**Resolved 2026-09-17 (technical confirmation, no number):** the wrapper described above should reuse the *same* flavor-based Capacitor mechanism already used for SOCIII's own flagship apps, not a second bespoke system — "the same, with a wrapper," at least for now.

**Revised 2026-09-17 (#4 and #7) — the mechanical gate, and a real distinction the first draft blurred:**

**Action risk and content risk are different things, and only one of them is bounded by capability scoping.** The first draft claimed Tier A "does the real regulatory-exposure work, structurally" by never granting a creator worker a capability that touches money-movement or health data directly. The red-team pass correctly caught that this is only true for *system-action* risk. A bare `creator.chat` capability — the most basic, obviously-necessary thing any creator worker needs — is entirely sufficient for that worker to dispense harmful medical, legal, or financial advice in plain text. No write-access capability is required for that risk to exist at all. Given SOCIII's own vertical roster (nursing, mortgage servicing, real estate) skews toward exactly these regulated-adjacent domains, creators drawn to this platform will skew the same way. Tier A does not touch this risk. Nothing in the original draft did.

Sean's answer to #7 is that liability for a creator's own domain content should sit with the creator, not SOCIII — but per the revision note above, that's an intent pending real legal work, not a mechanism that makes the content risk disappear today. Given that, the gate is:

- **Tier A — auto-approved, fully mechanical, bounds actions only.** A creator worker may only declare capabilities from a new, deliberately narrow `creator.*` class — content generation, chat, scheduling, read-only lookups on the creator's own content. No write access to another tenant's data, no direct financial-transaction capability, no direct PHI-adjacent capability. The check is a set-membership test: every capability referenced exists in the allowlist, and the worker defines zero new capability IDs of its own. It then inherits the same `requiredKyc`/`requiredRoles`/tenant-scoping enforcement every other capability already gets platform-wide. **What this tier actually does:** prevents a creator worker from directly moving money or touching another party's health data through the platform's own systems, and keeps billing/account handling inside SOCIII's own already-compliant Stripe/account infrastructure. **What it does not do:** say anything about the safety or accuracy of what the worker says in conversation. Both are true; the first draft only said the first one.
- **Tier B — two genuinely different jobs, not one.** (1) A platform-integrity/abuse scan — impersonation of SOCIII/Anthropic/a real person, prompt-injection/self-jailbreak attempts, runtime attempts to act outside declared capability scope. This part is real, mechanical, and uncontroversial. (2) **Regulated-domain content flagging — reinstated at submission time, not dropped.** The first draft removed this on the theory that domain-content correctness isn't SOCIII's kuleana to check; round 1's red-team pass is right that removing the flag entirely was a risk *transfer* dressed up as a simplification, not a clean one. The flag doesn't need to adjudicate whether the content is *correct* — that genuinely isn't a scalable or appropriate thing for an automated gate to decide, and stays the creator's domain per #7's intent. What it does: detect medical/legal/financial-advice-shaped language at submission time.
  - **Round 2 correction — detection alone was worse than either extreme, not a safe middle ground.** The round-1 design had a detected flag trigger only a B2B creator acknowledgment. Round 2 correctly caught that this establishes SOCIII's actual knowledge of the risk while establishing no corresponding safeguard — a worse negligence/UDAAP posture than not detecting it at all, or detecting it and actually doing something. A detected flag now has to produce **both** of the following, not just the internal acknowledgment: **(a) a subscriber-facing disclosure** — a persistent, visible statement in that worker's chat surface (e.g., "This is an AI tool, not a licensed [medical/legal/financial] professional; for informational purposes only") shown to the actual end user, not just recorded between SOCIII and the creator — this is the piece round 1 was missing entirely, and it protects the subscriber, not just SOCIII's paper trail; **(b) an ongoing monitoring cadence**, not a one-time checkbox — a flagged worker gets periodically re-sampled (not necessarily every conversation, but on a real, non-zero, non-complaint-reactive schedule), so "we detected it" is followed by "and we kept watching," not "and we logged it once."
  - **Round 2 correction — this can't be submission-time-only, or it's trivially evaded.** A creator's worker is not fully described by its declared capabilities — its system prompt/instructions can be edited post-approval without touching any capability declaration at all. As drafted, a worker could pass Tier A/B on a benign "wellness chat" description, then have its prompt edited into dispensing specific medical dosing advice, with nothing in this gate ever seeing the change. **Fix: every edit to a creator worker's system prompt/instructions (not just its capability list) re-runs Tier A and Tier B before the edit is live to real subscribers** — this is the most direct evasion path in the round-1 design and it wasn't mentioned at all.
- **Tier C — not self-serve.** Any request for a capability outside `creator.*`, or a genuinely new capability, still routes to a real human decision — which in practice overlaps with the graduation conversation (Resolved #2) and shouldn't pretend to be mechanical.

**This remains a business decision Sean needs to make explicitly, not one this doc should resolve unilaterally:** is Tier B's reinstated content-flag-plus-subscriber-disclosure-plus-monitoring step acceptable, or does Sean want to formally accept the content risk with no flag at all (the original first-draft position), understanding that choice is currently backed by intent language and no insurance? Recorded as reopened, not decided, in §5.

**Why this self-selects correctly:** most creators will be satisfied with a web storefront reaching their whole audience without any app-store friction. Only creators serious enough to run their own developer account will bother with the native option — which is exactly the population equipped to carry that overhead, rather than SOCIII carrying it on their behalf.

**Layer 3 stays separate and rare.** A creator's work becoming a fully SOCIII-built, SOCIII-published flagship app is a deliberate investment decision SOCIII initiates (based on revenue, strategic fit, or exclusivity) — never something a creator graduates into by request or by hitting a metric threshold on their own.

### 4.1 Post-publish enforcement — entirely missing from the first draft

The red-team pass correctly caught that everything above describes what gets *approved*. Nothing in the first draft described what happens after: a creator worker later found to be scamming subscribers, infringing IP, impersonating a licensed professional, or generating a pattern of subscriber complaints. That gap needs a real answer before this framework goes live, not just an acknowledgment here — but naming the pieces:

- **Complaint intake and audit trigger.** A subscriber complaint against a creator worker needs a defined path to a human, with a response-time expectation — not something that only surfaces if it happens to escalate.
- **Suspension and appeal.** A creator worker needs a real "pull it now, argue about it after" suspension mechanism (mirroring the KYC review-queue accept/reject shape already in `services/idVerification.js`), plus a defined appeal path back the other way.
- **IP/trademark clearance at intake, not just capability scope.** A creator publishing "under their own name" is a real trademark and impersonation surface (does this name/brand actually belong to this person, does it collide with an existing mark) that Tier A/B as designed don't check at all.
- **A counterweight to revenue share's own incentive.** SOCIII takes a cut of every creator's revenue — which means whoever reviews or investigates a marginal creator has a structural incentive to wave them through, not flag them. Whatever function ends up doing Tier B/C review or handling complaints should not be compensated in a way that's sensitive to the revenue it's reviewing, and should have a minimum audit-sampling rate independent of complaint volume, not just a complaint-reactive one.

None of this is designed yet. It's Open Decision #8.

### 4.2 Two more gaps found on round 2 — who the creator is, and whose data this is

- **Creator identity/payout KYC is not the same thing as capability-level `requiredKyc`, and this doc conflated them.** Tier A inherits `requiredKyc` from the capability registry — that governs what a *worker* is allowed to call. It says nothing about verifying who a *creator* actually is before they start collecting subscriber payments through SOCIII's Stripe rail and receiving a revenue-share payout. Identity verification and sanctions/OFAC screening for a creator-as-payee is a distinct, effectively mandatory requirement — if the payout rail is Stripe Connect or equivalent, it likely forces some of this already, but this doc shouldn't assume that dependency silently. It needs to be confirmed and stated as an explicit gate. Open Decision #9.
- **No determination of who's the data controller for subscriber conversation data flowing through a creator's storefront.** Once a subscriber chats with a creator's worker, SOCIII's infrastructure is processing that data on behalf of a creator-branded storefront. Under GDPR-style frameworks — relevant, not hypothetical, given SOCIII's existing EU footprint (the Estonia entity work and EU data-residency track already in motion) — that's a real controller/processor/joint-controller question with breach-notification and subject-access-request implications. Untouched anywhere in this doc until now. Open Decision #10.

The whole gate now also re-runs on every post-approval edit to a worker's prompt/instructions, not just at initial submission (round 2 — the most direct evasion path in round 1's design). **Still open:** whether Sean wants this reinstated, higher-teeth flag, or wants to formally accept zero content screening — see the explicit callout in §4.

7. **Liability/support boundary — reopened. This is the load-bearing item in the whole doc and it is not resolved.** Sean's intent (liability for a creator's domain content sits with the creator) is recorded, but nothing operative backs it yet: no ToS clause, no indemnification agreement, no insurance conversation, no counsel review — and SOCIII currently carries no cyber/tech E&O coverage at all. **This needs actual legal work before any of §4 is treated as a settled framework, not just a documented intent.**
8. **Post-publish enforcement — new (round 1), entirely unaddressed in the first draft.** Complaint intake, suspension/appeal, IP/trademark clearance at intake, and a structural counterweight to revenue share's incentive to wave marginal creators through (see §4.1). Nothing designed yet.

10. **Data controller/processor role for subscriber chat data — new (round 2).** A real GDPR-shaped question given SOCIII's existing EU footprint, not hypothetical. Undesigned (§4.2).

**Bottom line, sharpened by round 2:** the technical taxonomy (layers, Capacitor flavors, capability registry) is well-grounded and repo-verified. The liability architecture — #7 above all, with #4, #8, #9, and #10 as its direct downstream consequences — is the actual unresolved core of this doc. Round 2's own framing is the right one to close on: the residual risk isn't just that the gate has gaps, it's that a point-in-time submission check on something (a creator's prompt/behavior) that can drift continuously after approval needs continuous, not one-time, controls — and the safeguards recorded here need to actually protect the subscriber and SOCIII's legal position, not just produce a paper trail.
