## 1. Executive Summary

- **Our existing ADS-B integration cannot answer this question, and no login would fix that.** `functions/functions/services/aviation/adsb.js`, the connector currently wired into SKYE, calls ADS-B Exchange's **RapidAPI-hosted live product** (confirmed via `docs/API-Registry-Session41.md` and the `.env` config's `ADSB_EXCHANGE_RAPIDAPI_HOST` setting) — a live radius-around-a-point query only, with zero historical or by-registration lookup. This is a product-tier limitation, not an access problem.
- **The real gap is bigger than one report.** `raas/aviation/GLOBAL/AV-032-crew-scheduling-rules.md` (the existing Crew Scheduling & Roster worker spec) treats `minimum_staffing_levels` as an **operator-configured input**, not something derived from actual historical mission-launch patterns. There is no heatmap, trend, or utilization-analytics capability anywhere in the aviation vertical today — confirmed by grep, not assumed absent.
- **A real, separate ADS-B Exchange product likely fits this better than raw position traces:** their **"Daily Operations"** product is described as "a comprehensive, BI-ready daily feed of completed flight events... from origin to destination" — i.e., pre-computed wheels-up/wheels-down legs, not something we'd need to reconstruct ourselves from raw position data. This is meaningfully less engineering work than the raw-trace approach originally discussed, if the product actually delivers what its description implies (unverified — no public pricing or query-by-registration confirmation; see §4).
- **Two real, useful data sources already exist in the platform that this can cross-reference, not just the new ADS-B feed:** `dispatchTripRequests/{scopeId}/requests/{id}` already stores a real `requestedDepartureZulu` (planned/dispatched departure time) per trip. Comparing that to the *actual* ADS-B-verified wheels-up time could produce a differentiated "planned vs. actual launch drift" metric, not just a raw heatmap of when planes happen to fly — **but this depends entirely on dispatchers actually filling the field in.** It's optional today (no required-field validation, unlike `destination`), and this doc has not checked its real-world fill rate. Treat "differentiated metric" as a real possibility contingent on that check, not a confirmed capability.
- **The commercial data product is sold as an annual-commitment subscription, not pay-per-query** — ADS-B Exchange's own product page states their data products are "delivered as ongoing subscription services with minimum annual commitments." No public pricing exists; this needs a direct vendor quote before any cost estimate here is more than a guess. Treat this as a real, unresolved cost unknown, not a rounding error.
- **The flights that never happened are as informative as the ones that did — a real addition to this doc's scope, per Sean.** `dispatchTripRequests` records intent (a trip was requested/released); ADS-B records fact (a flight actually occurred). A trip request with `status: "released"` that never shows up as a matching ADS-B flight is a real, detectable gap — and it's ambiguous in a useful way: it could mean an operational failure (aircraft down for MX, no crew available — directly relevant to the shift-timing question) or a missed market opportunity (real demand the operator couldn't serve, relevant to a charter operator's growth case). See §5 for how this cross-reference works and §6 for the real matching-logic questions it raises.
- **A third real data source (WeatherTurndown.com, added mid-review) could resolve the "why wasn't this flown" question §5 otherwise leaves to human judgment — but confirmed as a per-customer conditional integration, not something SOCIII can source once and reuse.** LFN has an account, but it's company-level access Sean can't use himself even with his own LFN pilot standing — meaning this can only ever be an opt-in feature for customers who are themselves already WeatherTurndown participants, connected via their own credentials, never a general SOCIII-side data source. See §4 and Open Decision #10. Per Sean: fulfilled flights (ADS-B) + weather-turned-down flights (WeatherTurndown, for participating customers) + unexplained gaps (this doc's own detection) + pilot/MX cross-referencing together are a genuinely fuller operational picture than ForeFlight or comparable tools show today — a real differentiator, contingent on the open questions in §4/§6 actually resolving favorably.
- **This has real cross-vertical product potential beyond LFN's staffing question** — Sean's own framing (EMS staffing, charter operator utilization, individual pilot performance logging, and the observation that Part 135 ops software generally lacks trend analytics without heavy custom reporting) suggests this is a genuine differentiator worth building as a SKYE capability, not a one-off internal report. That framing is Sean's own strategic read, not independently verified against competitor products in this pass — worth a real competitive check before leaning on it in a sales narrative.

## 2. Current Platform State (Audit)

### 2.1 Existing ADS-B connector — live-only, confirmed by both code and account documentation

`functions/functions/services/aviation/adsb.js` implements `GET /v1/aviation:traffic?lat=&lon=&dist=`, calling ADS-B Exchange's live `v2/lat/{lat}/lon/{lon}/dist/{nm}` endpoint. `getTraffic()` takes no date parameter and no registration parameter for a specific tail's history — by design, per the code's own comment, it is "the 'radar returns' for the dispatch / flight-following / map views," i.e., a live snapshot.

The `.env` config sets `ADSB_EXCHANGE_RAPIDAPI_HOST`, meaning this specific key is provisioned through RapidAPI's hosted product (confirmed against `docs/API-Registry-Session41.md:34`: *"Via RapidAPI - live aircraft tracking"*). RapidAPI's ADS-B Exchange product does not offer a historical endpoint at any tier — this is a different product from ADS-B Exchange's own direct historical data line (§4), not a permissions issue on our existing key.

### 2.2 A real "planned departure time" data source already exists

`functions/functions/services/dispatch/tripRequests.js` implements a real, non-demo Firestore collection: `dispatchTripRequests/{scopeId}/requests/{id}`, with a `requestedDepartureZulu` field (line 70) — a genuine planned-departure timestamp per trip, entered at dispatch time. This is a real, live-written field, not a demo/seed artifact (confirmed distinct from the real-estate vertical's disconnected-demo-data pattern found in CODEX 90 — this one is part of the actual trip-request write path).

### 2.3 Duty-time tracking exists but is not wired to any automatic flight-leg source

`functions/functions/services/copilot/logic/dutyTimeTracker.js` computes FAR Part 135 duty/flight-time legality (`computeDutyStatus()`) from `dutyPeriods` and `logEntries` Firestore data — real regulatory math (8hr/24hr flight time, 14/16hr duty windows, 7/30/365-day rolling limits). It consumes whatever duty-period and logbook data already exists; it has no mechanism today to cross-check those manually-entered records against independently-verified ADS-B flight legs. Not a gap this doc needs to close, but a plausible downstream consumer once reconstructed legs exist (see §5).

### 2.4 Crew scheduling treats staffing levels as a configured input, not a data-derived output — this is the actual gap

`raas/aviation/GLOBAL/AV-032-crew-scheduling-rules.md` is a real, detailed worker spec for crew scheduling (legality pre-validation via AV-009, trade/giveaway handling, PTO, reserve policy). Its `minimum_staffing_levels` field (Tier 2, Company Policies) is explicitly operator-configured — "Minimum number of qualified pilots that must be on duty at each base for each shift... Configurable." **Nothing in this spec, or anywhere else in the aviation vertical, derives that number (or a recommended shift start/stop time) from actual historical mission-launch patterns.** This confirms Sean's own read of the gap — the crew-scheduling worker manages a roster against a staffing target, it does not help set that target from real operational data.

### 2.5 No existing analytics/heatmap capability anywhere comparable

Grepped `functions/functions/services/aviation`, `services/mx`, and every canvas component under `apps/business/src/components/canvas` for `heatmap`/`analytics`/`trend` — zero real matches (the file-name hits returned by the grep, e.g. `ChartCard.jsx`, are generic chart-rendering components used across unrelated verticals, not an existing mission-analytics feature).

## 3. The Use Cases (per Sean, not independently verified against competitors)

1. **EMS/HEMS operator staffing (the originating example)** — at Life Flight Network specifically, per Sean, there is currently no tracking anywhere that produces a heat-graph of actual mission launch times. This directly affects shift-start-time decisions (7am vs. 8am vs. 9am base coverage) and, per Sean, has "a huge impact on staffing and aircraft MX issues" — MX scheduling presumably benefits from knowing real utilization windows (when the aircraft is actually flying vs. sitting), not just flight-hour totals.
2. **Charter/Part 135 operator utilization and staffing** — same underlying need generalizes beyond EMS to any Part 135 operator trying to right-size crew coverage against actual demand patterns rather than assumed/static schedules.
3. **Individual pilot performance/currency flight logging** — a reconstructed, independently-verified flight history (not self-reported) could feed a pilot's own currency/logbook tooling, cross-checking self-logged time against ADS-B-verified legs.
4. **Unfulfilled-mission detection (Sean, added mid-review) — a distinct use case from the launch-time heatmap.** Cross-referencing every `released` trip request against the ADS-B-verified flight record surfaces requests that were never actually flown. Two very different real meanings hide behind the same signal: an operational failure (no aircraft/crew available when needed — directly actionable for the staffing/shift question in item 1) or unmet market demand (a charter operator turning away or failing to serve real demand — a growth/capacity business case, not just an ops one). Distinguishing the two requires more than the raw gap itself (see §6, item 9) — this is a real, valuable signal, but the "why" behind each gap isn't handed to you for free.
5. **Competitive differentiation** — Sean's framing: "trend data is lacking from pretty much all the Part 135 RAAS out there unless you really know how to generate custom reports." This is a real, plausible product angle given what CODEX 23/CODEX 90's audits already found elsewhere in the platform (specs exist, trend/analytics layers generally don't) — but it's Sean's own market read stated in this conversation, not something this doc independently confirmed against actual competitor product documentation. Worth a real competitive check before it's used in outward-facing positioning.

## 4. Data Sourcing Options — real products, real unknowns

**None of these three is a settled recommendation — Option A is named first because its described shape fits best, not because it's the most certain path.** Option A currently has zero confirmed pricing and zero confirmed query-by-registration capability; Option C, by contrast, is fully accessible today with only a license-terms check outstanding. If the Option A vendor conversation stalls or the product doesn't actually support per-registration date-range queries, **Option C is the real, available-now fallback**, not a last resort.

**Option A — ADS-B Exchange's "Daily Operations" product.** Described on their own site as "a comprehensive, BI-ready daily feed of completed flight events, delivering the clearest global view of aircraft activity from origin to destination." If this genuinely delivers pre-computed per-leg wheels-up/wheels-down events by registration, it removes most of the leg-reconstruction engineering work Option B and C require. **Unverified: no public pricing, and their own page does not confirm query-by-registration or a specific date-range-per-tail access pattern** — this needs a direct sales conversation, not an assumption.

**Option B — ADS-B Exchange's raw "Trace Files" / "Daily Positions" product.** Individual aircraft position traces (24-hour periods, or high-resolution 2x/second). This is the same underlying data shape as the free mirror in Option C — buying it gets commercial support/SLA and a cleaner delivery mechanism ("pulled from buckets" or "pushed to customer-owned cloud storage" per their site), but **we would still have to build the wheels-up/wheels-down leg-reconstruction logic ourselves** (detect ground→airborne→ground transitions from altitude/speed data) — real, non-trivial engineering distinct from just having the data.

**Option C — the free `adsb.lol` community mirror (`globe_history_2024`/`globe_history_2025` GitHub releases).** Openly licensed, zero cost, same underlying trace-file format as Option B. Organized as **full-day global archives** (every tracked aircraft worldwide, per day) rather than clean per-tail files — extracting two specific tails means processing large global dumps repeatedly, and there's no commercial support if something breaks. **The "openly licensed" claim needs verification against the actual license terms before relying on this as a permanent commercial data source** — fine for prototyping, not yet confirmed fine for a paid product feature.

**A real, low-cost validation path exists regardless of which option is chosen long-term:** ADS-B Exchange's own site states "complete data from the 1st of each month are made available free of charge" for evaluation. That's enough to prototype the full pipeline — sourcing, leg reconstruction (if needed), and heatmap output — against one real day of data for N661LF/N662LF before committing to any paid subscription. **Recommended first step regardless of the final sourcing decision — with two success criteria beyond "does the pipeline run," per Sean's review:**
1. **Coverage quality at the actual altitudes/terrain these tails fly.** ADS-B Exchange's coverage (crowdsourced ground receivers plus satellite) is strongest for high-altitude en-route traffic and gets patchier for low-level flight over mountainous or sparsely-populated terrain — exactly the profile of HEMS operations around the Hawaiian bases this is meant to analyze. The free evaluation day should confirm real coverage for N661LF/N662LF's actual routes, not just that the API responds.
2. **Confirmed continuous ADS-B Out equipage for the full requested lookback window.** If either aircraft's transponder setup changed, was down for maintenance, or the aircraft is newer than the requested 12-month window, a purchased year of history could have silent holes. Verify equipage history before buying, not after.

**Both apply to Option C even for internal-only use, not just if this becomes a paid feature for other tenants — worth restating plainly:** if SOCIII builds real shift-planning recommendations on data whose license terms haven't been confirmed, that's a live question the moment it informs an actual staffing decision, not only once it's sold. Resolve the license question before the free mirror is used for anything beyond the one-day prototype, even internally.

**A fourth, distinct data source (Sean, added mid-review): WeatherTurndown.com — directly relevant to Open Decision #9's "why wasn't this flown" question, and confirmed as a per-customer conditional integration, not a general SOCIII data source.** Researched this session: it's a real, existing industry tool — "a free service allowing medical transport programs to share current information regarding delays or cancellations due to weather," per its own site. Real characteristics worth knowing before relying on it: **login-gated to a company-level account** — LFN has one, but per Sean it's company access he can't use himself even with his own LFN pilot standing, confirming this can only ever work as an opt-in integration where a participating customer connects their own account, never something SOCIII sources once and reuses across tenants. It's also **self-reported by participating programs** rather than automated/independently verified (a different trust level than ADS-B-derived data), and **focused on real-time/current turndowns** — no API or historical-data-retention period is documented on its public site, and this can only be checked once an actual participating customer is involved in building the integration. If it does have queryable history, cross-referencing it against `detectUnfulfilledMissions` output would let the platform classify at least the weather-caused subset of unfulfilled missions objectively, for customers who participate, rather than leaving 100% of that classification to human judgment as Open Decision #9 currently assumes for everyone else. **This is also the piece that makes the differentiation claim in §3 item 5 concrete, per Sean's own framing**: fulfilled flights (ADS-B) + requested-but-weather-turned-down (WeatherTurndown, where the customer participates) + requested-but-unfulfilled-for-other-reasons (the gap this doc's own detection surfaces) + pilot/MX cross-referencing (§2.3's `dutyTimeTracker`, §2.4's crew-scheduling) is a genuinely fuller picture than what ForeFlight or similar tools show today — worth treating as a real, connected differentiator, not four separate features.

**Shared, unresolved cost fact:** ADS-B Exchange's commercial products are sold as "ongoing subscription services with minimum annual commitments," not pay-per-query like our existing live connector. This is a materially different cost shape than the ~$0.002/query line item already in `VENDORS.md` for the live connector — a real quote is needed before this can be sized as a line-item cost, and the annual-commitment structure itself is worth weighing against the free-tier evaluation data before signing anything.

## 5. Proposed Capability & Architecture (pending Option A/B/C decision)

- **The wheels-up/wheels-down leg-reconstruction logic (Options B/C) is materially harder for EMS missions than for point-to-point commercial traffic — this doc's original framing understated that.** A HEMS launch can involve a scene landing, a hover/hoist without a full touchdown, a hospital transfer, and sometimes a repositioning leg. A naive ground→airborne→ground detector (built with commercial point-to-point flights in mind) could misclassify a single multi-stop mission as several unrelated flights, or miss a hoist operation entirely since it may never register "on ground" at all. This directly undermines the planned-vs-actual and unfulfilled-mission metrics below if legs are split or merged incorrectly — real reconstruction-logic design work, not a detail to defer past the prototype stage. (Option A's pre-computed events, if they hold up, may sidestep this — another reason to run that vendor conversation rather than defaulting to Option B/C engineering.)
- **New capability namespace**, extending the existing `aviation.*` pattern rather than inventing a new one: something like `aviation.get_mission_history_v1` (per-tail, date range → list of reconstructed/sourced legs with wheels-up/wheels-down/duration) and `aviation.get_launch_time_heatmap_v1` (aggregated by hour-of-day/day-of-week, the actual shift-planning output Sean asked for).
- **Store reconstructed/sourced legs once, not on every report view.** A new Firestore collection (or a subcollection under the existing `aircraftRecords/{scopeId}/aircraft/{tailNumber}` structure, consistent with how squawks/logbook entries are already scoped) populated via a one-time historical backfill plus a daily incremental job — avoids re-fetching/re-parsing the same data repeatedly.
- **Cross-reference with `dispatchTripRequests.requestedDepartureZulu` (§2.2), if the field's real fill rate justifies it** — comparing planned-vs-actual launch times would be more differentiated than raw launch-time distribution alone, per §1, but this is contingent on dispatchers actually populating an optional field, not yet confirmed. Check the real fill rate before designing this as a headline feature rather than a nice-to-have if the data supports it.
- **Unfulfilled-mission detection**, per §3 item 4: for every `dispatchTripRequests` record with `status: "released"` (see `tripRequests.js`'s `VALID_STATUSES`), check for a matching ADS-B-verified leg on the requested tail within a reasonable time window of `requestedDepartureZulu`. No match = a real, flaggable gap. This is additive to the heatmap/drift capabilities above, not a replacement — it needs its own matching-window logic (§6, item 9) since "no exact-time match" and "genuinely never flown" aren't automatically the same thing (a delayed-but-flown mission and a truly cancelled one both need to be told apart before this is trustworthy).
- **Plausible, later downstream consumers — not in scope for a v1 build:** AV-032's `minimum_staffing_levels` could eventually shift from a purely operator-configured value to a data-informed recommendation; `dutyTimeTracker.js` could eventually cross-check manually-logged duty periods against independently-verified legs. Both are real possibilities worth naming so the data model doesn't foreclose them, but neither should be assumed as committed scope right now.

## 6. Open Decisions for Sean

1. **Data source (§4, Option A/B/C)** — get a real quote from ADS-B Exchange for the "Daily Operations" product specifically (confirm it actually supports per-registration, date-ranged queries before assuming it does), and weigh the annual-commitment cost shape against the free `adsb.lol` mirror's engineering cost and unverified license terms. Recommend running the free-evaluation-day prototype (§4) before this decision is made, not instead of it.
2. **Historical depth and backfill scope** — 12 months for two tails is the originating ask; is the real target the whole LFN/charter fleet, and how far back does a useful analysis actually need to go (a full year captures seasonal patterns; less might already show the daily/weekly launch-time pattern that actually drives shift-start decisions)?
3. **Data sensitivity / who sees it** — aircraft movement history is operationally sensitive (a competitor or adversary could infer base coverage patterns or predict aircraft availability). Confirm this stays strictly tenant-scoped, and treat any future idea of cross-tenant/anonymized industry benchmarking (a plausible upsell given the "trend data is lacking industry-wide" framing in §3) as its own separate decision requiring real privacy/competitive-sensitivity review, not an assumed extension of this feature.
4. **Labor/union sensitivity — noted, explicitly out of scope for SOCIII to gate.** EMS/HEMS pilots are frequently unionized, and using independently-verified tracking data to justify shift-length or start-time changes is workplace-surveillance-adjacent. Per Sean (2026-09-16): this is the client's own call to make, not something SOCIII negotiates against itself on or holds up feature delivery for — flagged here so an operator has the information, not as a build-blocking condition.
5. **Liability/discoverability — a distinct concern from the labor item above, for the operator's safety/legal side, not HR.** Once independently-verified launch-time data is formally built into a staffing/shift-planning tool, that pipeline becomes part of the paper trail around scheduling decisions. If a fatigue-related incident is ever investigated, a tool that informed or recommended a shift schedule is a materially different discovery target than an ops manager's informal judgment call. Worth a line to whoever handles an operator's safety/legal function — separate from, and not a substitute for, item 4's labor-relations note.
6. **ADS-B coverage quality and equipage continuity (§4)** — confirm real coverage at N661LF/N662LF's actual low-altitude/remote-terrain routes, and confirm both tails were continuously ADS-B Out-equipped and transmitting for the full requested lookback window, before committing to any paid annual subscription. Both are cheap to check during the free-evaluation-day prototype and expensive to discover after signing.
7. **EMS-specific leg-reconstruction design (§5)** — scene landings, hover/hoist operations without full touchdown, and multi-stop missions need explicit handling in the ground/airborne detection logic (if Option B/C is chosen) before the planned-vs-actual and unfulfilled-mission metrics can be trusted. Don't assume a commercial-traffic-shaped detector works for HEMS missions unchanged.
8. **Scope of v1** — just the launch-time heatmap for shift planning (the original ask), or the fuller planned-vs-actual drift analysis (§5) in the same release? The latter is more differentiated but depends on trip-request data discipline (dispatchers actually filling in `requestedDepartureZulu` consistently) that hasn't been separately verified in this pass.
9. **Unfulfilled-mission matching logic and interpretation (§3 item 4, §5)** — what time window counts as "no match" (a mission delayed 2 hours and flown late is not the same finding as one truly never flown)? And once a real gap is detected, is distinguishing "operational failure" from "missed market demand" something the data itself can support (e.g., cross-referencing against the existing `status: "cancelled"` field and any recorded cancellation reason), or does it need a human to actually look at each flagged gap? Don't assume the platform can auto-classify the "why" — that's a real open question, not an implementation detail.
10. **WeatherTurndown.com integration (§4) — confirmed per-tenant, not something SOCIII can source once and reuse.** Per Sean: LFN does have an account, but it's company-level access — not something Sean can use himself even with his own LFN pilot standing, and not something SOCIII can validate or prototype against right now. This means WeatherTurndown integration can only ever be a feature available to customers who are themselves already WeatherTurndown member-program participants, gated by their own credentials into their own account — never a SOCIII-wide data source. Real, still worth building for the operators who do participate, but scope this as a conditional/opt-in integration point (the customer connects their own WeatherTurndown access), not a capability the platform can validate or ship generally. Whether the service exposes queryable history or only a live map remains genuinely unconfirmed and can only be checked once an actual participating customer is involved.
11. **Competitive positioning claim (§3, item 5)** — "trend data is lacking from pretty much all the Part 135 RAAS out there" is Sean's own market read; worth a real, dedicated competitive-product check before it's used in outward-facing sales/marketing material, the same way CODEX 74's competitor teardown grounded the DPP vertical's differentiation claims rather than asserting them.
