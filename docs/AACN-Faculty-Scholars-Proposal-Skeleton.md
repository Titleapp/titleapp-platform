## Abstract / Project Summary (≤ 150 words)

Clinical nursing education relies on paper evaluations, siloed LMS grade books, and faculty memory to document student competency development — a fragmented system that produces no portable, verifiable record of what a student has learned or who signed off on it. This pilot study examines whether an AI-assisted integrated learning record platform, in which faculty-signed competency evaluations are captured as immutable, append-only records that students retain beyond graduation, produces measurable differences in clinical competency outcomes compared to standard institutional documentation practice.

## Background and Significance (≤ 400 words of the 1,200-word body)

Accreditation bodies (ACEN, CCNE) require nursing programs to demonstrate sustained assessment of student competency — yet the systems used to capture that competency are neither portable nor longitudinal. When a student changes programs, transfers clinical sites, or graduates, the granular record of their competency development does not travel with them. Employers and licensing boards receive a transcript with course grades; the underlying evidence — specific skills signed off by a named preceptor, clinical hours in each category, faculty evaluation scores — is effectively lost.

Emerging AI-assisted platforms offer a structural alternative: capturing each clinical competency event as a discrete, signed, append-only record that is owned by the student and readable by faculty, preceptors, advisors, and employers with explicit consent. Unlike an LMS grade book entry (editable, institution-owned, archived at graduation), this model treats the learning record as a chain of attested events — analogous to the electronic health record the student will document in clinical practice.

The *Journal of Professional Nursing* and *Nurse Education Today* have published multiple studies examining technology interventions in nursing education, but few address the structural question of who owns and retains the student record. This pilot addresses a gap: whether a platform designed around record ownership, AI coaching, and digitally signed attestation produces better documented competency outcomes than standard practice.

## Purpose Statement

This pilot study evaluates whether an AI-assisted integrated learning record platform produces measurable differences in clinical competency outcomes — including competency rating scores, clinical hours documented, and faculty evaluation completeness — compared to a concurrent comparison group using standard institutional record-keeping.

### Intervention

Students in the intervention arm will use the SOCIII AI-assisted learning record platform throughout the **[semester/academic year]**. The platform captures:

- Faculty clinical evaluations — digitally signed, written into the student's portable record as an immutable entry
- Clinical competency ratings — faculty-scored on a 1–5 scale (Novice → Expert) per learning objective
- Clinical hours — per shift, with preceptor attestation, by clinical category
- Assessment scores — integrated from **[ATI / specify source]** assessments
- Course grades — entered by faculty and retained in the student's portable record

Students in the comparison group complete the same clinical curriculum using standard Makai institutional documentation (LMS grade book, paper evaluations, and existing clinical records systems).

### Procedures

1. IRB approval or exempt determination obtained prior to any enrollment.
2. Written informed consent obtained from all students prior to assignment and prior to any data collection.
3. Students formally enrolled in the research arm via a platform consent event (recorded as an immutable logbook entry before any outcome data is collected).
4. Intervention runs for **[one semester / academic year]**.
5. Outcome data extracted via platform export at the end of the study period.

### Outcome Measures

- **Primary:** Average clinical competency rating (1–5 scale) per student at study end
- **Secondary:** Total clinical hours documented per student; faculty evaluation completion rate; assessment score percentile

### Analysis

Descriptive statistics and between-group comparisons (t-test or Mann-Whitney U depending on distribution) for primary and secondary outcomes. Sample size is a pilot constraint, not the result of a formal power calculation; this will be stated explicitly in the Limitations section.

## Limitations

- Single-site pilot; findings will not be generalizable without replication across multiple nursing programs.
- Quasi-experimental design with assignment by cohort rather than individual randomization limits causal claims.
- Sample size is constrained by program enrollment; formal power calculation is not possible for a single-cohort pilot.
- The intervention is a bundled platform (AI coaching, competency tracking, signed evaluations, record portability); outcome differences cannot be attributed to any single feature.
