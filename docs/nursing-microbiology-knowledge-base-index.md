## High-Yield Nursing Summary: Key Facts to Know

### Critical Drug-Bug Pairings
| Drug | Mechanism | Key Pathogens | Key Side Effects |
|------|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| Penicillin/Amoxicillin | β-lactam (cell wall) | Strep, syphilis, *Listeria* | Allergic reactions |
| Methicillin/Oxacillin | β-lactam (cell wall) | MSSA (not MRSA) | — |
| Ceftriaxone | 3rd-gen cephalosporin (cell wall) | Gonorrhea, meningitis, pneumonia | — |
| Vancomycin | Cell wall (peptide binding) | MRSA, VRE, C. diff (oral) | Nephrotoxic, Red Man Syndrome |
| Gentamicin | Aminoglycoside (30S ribosome) | Gram-negatives | Nephrotoxic, ototoxic |
| Tetracycline/Doxycycline | 30S ribosome | Chlamydia, Lyme, MRSA (skin) | Dental discoloration, phototoxic, avoid pregnancy |
| Azithromycin | 50S ribosome (macrolide) | Atypical pneumonia, STIs, pertussis | GI upset, QT prolongation |
| Clindamycin | 50S ribosome (lincosamide) | Anaerobes, MRSA skin | C. difficile colitis |
| Ciprofloxacin | DNA gyrase (fluoroquinolone) | UTIs, gram-negatives | Tendon rupture, QT prolongation |
| Isoniazid (INH) | Mycolic acid synthesis | *M. tuberculosis* | Hepatotoxic, peripheral neuropathy (give B6) |
| Rifampin | RNA polymerase | TB, meningococcal prophylaxis | Orange body fluids, many drug interactions |
| Metronidazole | DNA strand breaks | Anaerobes, *C. diff*, BV, *Trichomonas* | Metallic taste, disulfiram reaction with alcohol |
| TMP-SMX (Bactrim) | Folate synthesis (double block) | UTIs, PCP, MRSA (skin) | Sulfa allergy, bone marrow suppression |
| Oseltamivir (Tamiflu) | Neuraminidase inhibitor | Influenza A and B | Must take within 48 hours |
| Acyclovir | Viral DNA polymerase | HSV, VZV | Nephrotoxic (IV), maintain hydration |

### NEVER Give Antibiotics For:
- EHEC (*E. coli* O157:H7) → worsens HUS
- Viral infections (common cold, influenza unless bacterial superinfection)
- Most viral diarrhea (rotavirus, norovirus)

### DO NOT Use β-lactams For:
- MRSA (use vancomycin, linezolid, daptomycin)
- Mycoplasma (no cell wall — use macrolides or doxycycline)
- Chlamydia (intracellular — use doxycycline or azithromycin)
- *C. diff* as primary treatment (oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin preferred)

### Isolation/Precaution High-Yield Points
- **C. diff**: Contact precautions; soap and water handwashing (alcohol ineffective on spores)
- **MRSA/VRE**: Contact precautions; dedicated equipment
- **TB (active pulmonary)**: Airborne precautions (N95, negative-pressure room)
- **Measles/Varicella/Shingles (disseminated)**: Airborne precautions
- **Influenza**: Droplet precautions (surgical mask)
- **Norovirus**: Contact precautions; soap and water

### Gram Stain Quick Reference
| Gram-Positive | Gram-Negative |
|--------------|--------------|
| Thick peptidoglycan → stain purple/violet | Thin peptidoglycan + outer membrane → stain pink/red |
| *Staphylococcus* (clusters) | *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, *Shigella* (rods) |
| *Streptococcus* (chains) | *Pseudomonas* (rods) |
| *Clostridium* (rods, spores) | *Neisseria* (diplococci) |
| *Bacillus* (rods, spores) | *Haemophilus* (coccobacilli) |
| *Listeria* (rods) | *Vibrio* (curved rods) |
| *Mycobacterium* (acid-fast — special stain) | *Legionella* (poor Gram stain; use silver stain) |
| *Mycoplasma* (no cell wall — no Gram stain) | *Chlamydia* (intracellular — no Gram stain) |

### Vaccine-Preventable Diseases (Nursing High-Yield)
- **Hepatitis B**: 3 doses; birth, 1-2 months, 6 months; all healthcare workers
- **Influenza**: Annual; all healthcare workers
- **MMR**: 2 doses; live attenuated; contraindicated in pregnancy/immunocompromised
- **DTaP/Tdap**: 5 doses DTaP children; Tdap in pregnancy (27-36 weeks) to protect newborn from pertussis
- **Varicella**: 2 doses; check immunity before healthcare work
- **Pneumococcal**: PCV13 + PPSV23 for adults ≥65 and high-risk
- **Shingrix (Zoster)**: 2 doses; all adults ≥50; ~91% effective

### Key Disease-Complication Pairings
| Disease | Complication | Mechanism |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Strep throat | Rheumatic fever | Molecular mimicry (M protein cross-reacts with heart) |
| Strep throat | Glomerulonephritis | Immune complex deposition in glomeruli |
| Gonorrhea | PID → infertility | Salpingitis, fallopian tube scarring |
| Chlamydia | PID → ectopic pregnancy | Silent salpingitis |
| Campylobacter | Guillain-Barré syndrome | Post-infectious neuropathy |
| EHEC O157:H7 | Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome | Shiga toxin damages renal endothelium |
| Influenza | Secondary bacterial pneumonia | Damaged airway defenses |
| Measles | Encephalitis | Immune-mediated CNS damage |
| Chickenpox | Shingles (later in life) | VZV reactivation from dorsal ganglia |
| TB | Reactivation TB | Immunosuppression (HIV, steroids, TNF inhibitors) |
| Hepatitis B/C | Hepatocellular carcinoma | Chronic hepatic inflammation |
| Syphilis | Neurosyphilis, aortic aneurysm | Tertiary disease if untreated |

### Endotoxin vs. Exotoxin (Exam Favorite)
| Feature | Endotoxin (LPS) | Exotoxin |
|---------|----------------|---------|
| Bacteria source | Gram-NEGATIVE only | Mostly gram-POSITIVE (some gram-negative) |
| Chemical | Lipopolysaccharide | Protein |
| Heat | Heat STABLE | Heat LABILE (mostly) |
| Effect | General inflammation, fever, shock | Specific: cell death, flaccid/spastic paralysis, diarrhea |
| Examples | LPS from *E. coli*, *Pseudomonas*, *Salmonella* | Botulinum toxin, tetanus toxin, cholera toxin, TSST-1 |
| Treat | Supportive; antibiotics; LPS = trigger for septic shock | Antitoxins (neutralize); antibiotics; supportive |

### Botulinum vs. Tetanus (Confusing Pair)
| | Botulinum | Tetanus |
|-|-----------|---------|
| Organism | *C. botulinum* | *C. tetani* |
| Mechanism | Blocks ACh RELEASE at NMJ | Blocks inhibitory NTs (GABA, glycine) |
| Paralysis type | FLACCID (descending; starts with cranial nerves) | SPASTIC (ascending; starts with trismus/lockjaw) |
| Clinical | Drooping eyelids, diplopia, respiratory failure | Lockjaw, risus sardonicus, opisthotonus |
| Treatment | Antitoxin (heptavalent); supportive | Tetanus immune globulin; muscle relaxants; metronidazole; TIG |

### Important Normal Flora Location Reminders
- **Nasal passages**: *S. aureus* (20% carriage; risk for infection)
- **Throat**: Viridans streptococci, *S. pneumoniae* carriers
- **Skin**: *S. epidermidis*, *Corynebacterium*, *Cutibacterium*
- **Vagina**: Lactobacillus (protective; disruption → BV or yeast)
- **Large intestine**: *E. coli*, Bacteroides, Bifidobacterium
- **Note**: Any of the above become pathogenic when displaced (e.g., *E. coli* in urine)
