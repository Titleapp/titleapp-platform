## The One Rule

**Switzerland, not Disneyland.**

SOCIII sells trust. The product is an immutable record — cryptographically anchored, permanently auditable, governed by rules. The brand has to feel like that. Refined. Precise. Quietly confident. The kind of thing a serious operator would leave on a conference table without explanation.

Everything else in this guide follows from that.

## Voice and Tone

### What we sound like

- **Direct.** We state what the product does and what it costs. No hedging, no qualifiers, no "best-in-class."
- **Serious but not stiff.** We're talking to Chief Pilots and title attorneys and nursing program directors. They have no patience for hype. They respond to specificity.
- **Confident without overclaiming.** We have a pending patent application. We have live deployments. We have real customers. We say that — precisely, not expansively.
- **Picture-first.** We use numbers, tables, and before/after comparisons before we use adjectives.

### What we do not sound like

- No exclamation marks. Not in copy, not in email, not in presentations.
- No emoji in professional materials.
- No superlatives without evidence: not "the most powerful," "revolutionary," "industry-leading."
- No "we're disrupting" or "we're transforming" — show the disruption in the numbers, not in the language.
- No vague verbs: "streamline," "leverage," "empower," "unlock." Use the specific action.

### Naming conventions

| Term | Use | Do not use |
|---|---|---|
| **Alex** | Always just "Alex" | "the AI," "the bot," "the system" |
| **Chief of Staff** | Alex's role title | "operations coordinator," "AI coordinator," "assistant" |
| **Digital Workers** | User-facing name for AI agents | "bots," "automations," "AI tools" |
| **RAAS** | Internal/technical only | Use "Digital Workers" in all user-facing text |
| **Append-only record** | Technical description of the architecture | "blockchain database," "crypto ledger," "immutable DB" |
| **Patent-pending** | Status of USPTO filings (May 2026) | "patented," "we own the patent," "the patent covers" |

## Color Palette

### Brand colors (primary hierarchy)

| Tier | Name | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Purple | `#7C3AED` | Primary accent — section borders, links, highlights, eyebrow text, CTAs, headlines on dark surfaces |
| **Secondary** | Green | `#3E9B5F` | Secondary accent — supporting highlights, positive/favorable indicators (cost-comparison savings columns, favorable signals); used deliberately, not as often as Purple |
| **Tertiary** | Black | `#000000` | Dark-surface backgrounds (covers, presentation title slides); primary text on light surfaces where a true neutral is wanted |

Purple leads. Green is a deliberate secondary accent — use it where it signals something positive (a favorable number, a "you save" column), not as a general-purpose second fill competing with Purple. Black is for dark surfaces and high-contrast neutral text.

**Note on Green:** `#3E9B5F` was sampled from the logo mark at current available resolution. If a higher-resolution or vector version of the logo becomes available, re-sample and update this value. Treat the current hex as confirmed working, not final colorimetric.

### Supporting neutrals (functional utility, not brand-identity colors)

| Name | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| **Ink** | `#0f172a` | Headlines, primary text where full Black isn't specified |
| **Charcoal** | `#1a202c` | Body text |
| **Slate** | `#475569` | Subtitles, secondary body, captions |
| **Muted Slate** | `#64748b` | Labels, metadata, form labels |
| **Faint Slate** | `#94a3b8` | Footer text, timestamps, deemphasized content |
| **Rule** | `#cbd5e1` | Table borders, dividers |
| **Light Rule** | `#e2e8f0` | Section separators, subtle backgrounds |
| **Surface** | `#f8fafc` | Table header backgrounds, card fills |
| **White** | `#ffffff` | Page background |

### Dark surfaces (covers, presentation title slides)

Use **Dark Navy (`#0d1117`)** as the dark-surface background — this is what the actual brand lockup uses. Pure black (`#000000`) is harsher than the brand's dark background and makes the purple/green mark gradients read flat. The brand wordmark in white on Dark Navy is the correct cover treatment.

**What we do not use:**
- Gradients
- Neon or fluorescent colors
- Color backgrounds on body text pages (white only)
- Drop shadows on type
- Any color outside Purple / Green / Black as a primary brand accent — the supporting neutrals above are for utility only

## Typography

### Primary typeface — Helvetica Neue (or system equivalent: Arial)

Used for all brand-facing materials: headlines, subheadings, navigation, tables, labels, one-pagers, presentations.

| Role | Weight | Size (print) | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wordmark | 700 Bold | 28pt | Letter-spacing: 1.5px · Color: Black or Ink |
| Cover headline | 700 Bold | 30pt | Line-height: 1.15 · Color: White (on Black surface) or Ink (on white surface) |
| Section header | 700 Bold | 11pt | Uppercase · Letter-spacing: 0.5px · Border-bottom: Purple (`#7C3AED`) |
| Eyebrow / label | 600 SemiBold | 9pt | Uppercase · Letter-spacing: 2px · Color: Purple (`#7C3AED`) |
| Body | 400 Regular | 10–11pt | Line-height: 1.55 |
| Caption / metadata | 400 Regular | 8.5pt | Color: Muted Slate (`#64748b`) |

### Secondary typeface — Georgia (for legal and formal documents only)

Used in legal agreements, advisor/equity docs, and formal correspondence. Not used in sales materials, presentations, or product UI.

## Logo and Brand Mark

### The mark

Two interlocking angular bracket/chevron shapes. The left bracket faces right (purple, internal gradient from blue-purple to purple). The right bracket faces left (green, internal gradient from teal to darker green). Together they form a double-S or interlocking gear-like symbol. The shapes overlap at center with the green element slightly in front.

The mark works on both dark and white backgrounds. On dark backgrounds, the gradients read richest. On white, the mark reads clearly without modification — do not add a background box or shadow.

### The wordmark

"SOCIII" — white, widely tracked (generous letter-spacing), clean sans-serif. Not condensed. The three "I"s read as Roman numeral III, not the letters i-i-i. The wordmark always sits below the mark in vertical lockups and to the right in horizontal lockups.

### The tagline

**"COLLABORATIVE INTELLIGENCE ✳ PARTICIPATION"**

The official brand tagline. Used in full-lockup treatments (presentations, cover pages, hero sections). The asterisk/snowflake glyph (✳) separates the two phrases and matches the decorative divider element described below.

Not used in: one-pagers, email, short-form copy, or any context where it would feel forced. Use where the full brand identity is being established.

### The divider element

A horizontal rule that fades from Purple (`#7C3AED`) on the left to Green (`#3E9B5F`) on the right, with a centered asterisk/snowflake glyph (✳) interrupting it. Used on presentation covers and full-brand lockups below the wordmark, above the tagline. Do not use as a section divider in body copy — that role belongs to the simple Purple border-bottom on section headers.

### Dark background color

The brand lockup uses a very dark navy — approximately `#0d1117` to `#0f1724` — not pure black. When building presentation covers or dark-background sections, use this deep navy rather than `#000000`. Pure black reads as harsh in comparison to the purple/green mark; the dark navy lets the gradients breathe.

### Files

Located in `docs/specs/Investor-Memorandum-2026-05-25/`:

| File | Use |
|---|---|
| `brand-mark.svg` | Icon only — square format, for favicons, app icons, small lockups |
| `brand-wordmark.svg` | Full wordmark — horizontal, for document headers and email signatures |
| `brand-header.svg` / `.png` | Full-bleed header — for presentation covers, PDF covers, email headers |

### Usage rules

- **Minimum clear space:** equal to the height of the "S" in SOCIII on all sides.
- **On white backgrounds:** Ink (`#0f172a`) wordmark, or full-color logo mark.
- **On dark (navy) backgrounds:** White wordmark. Never use the dark wordmark on a dark surface.
- **Do not:** stretch, recolor, flatten the gradient to a solid, add drop shadows, or place the mark on busy photographic backgrounds.
- **Do not** use the wordmark at smaller than 100px wide in digital contexts.

## Writing Standards for Sales Materials

### Headlines

Lead with the customer's problem, not the product. Compare:

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| "SOCIII: AI Operations for Title Companies" | "Your closers are spending their time tracking documents, not closing." |
| "Revolutionize Your Aviation Operations" | "Your Chief Pilot shouldn't be the dispatch system." |

### One-pager structure

Every one-pager follows this order:
1. **The hook** — the specific, concrete problem (statement of fact, not a setup narrative)
2. **The problem** — why the current model is broken
3. **The wave** — why it is getting worse and cannot be stopped
4. **What SOCIII does** — 3–4 benefit-led bullets
5. **Financial comparison** — two-column table
6. **Force multiplier** — one before/after line
7. **The stack** — workers with record types
8. **Just talk to it** — verbatim Alex example
9. **CTA** — one clear next step
10. **Footer**

### Financial comparisons

Two-column table. Left: what they pay now (specific, named). Right: SOCIII. No ranges wider than 2:1.

| What you're paying now | SOCIII |
|---|---|
| Transaction coordinator: $45–65K/year | $150–250/month |
| CRM + tools: $3–8K/year | Included |

### The force multiplier statement

One before/after. Format: `[Role] + [what they're also doing] → [same role] + SOCIII`

### Just talk to it

Every one-pager ends with a verbatim Alex example. Italic for the user input.

*"I run a 5-aircraft charter operation out of Centennial. Which pilots are current this week?"*

Alex pulls all currency dates and flags anyone within 30 days of expiration. Before you finish the sentence.

## Presentation Deck Structure (10-Slide Standard)

| Slide | Title | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover | Brand header + headline stating the customer's problem |
| 2 | The Wave | Why this is happening now (not optional, not speculative) |
| 3 | The Old Model | What it costs and why it breaks |
| 4 | The SOCIII Answer | What the product actually does (outcomes, not features) |
| 5 | The Financial Case | Side-by-side cost comparison table |
| 6 | The Force Multiplier | Before/after statement + the math |
| 7 | The Architecture | Append-only record + blockchain anchoring in plain language |
| 8 | The Stack | Worker list with record types |
| 9 | The Pilot | Anonymized but specific |
| 10 | Just Talk to It | Three Alex conversation examples + CTA |

**Cover design:** Full-bleed Dark Navy (`#0d1117`) background. White wordmark + mark top-left. Headline in white 30pt Helvetica Neue Bold. Vertical name in Purple (`#7C3AED`) eyebrow treatment above headline. Optional: Purple-to-Green gradient divider with ✳ glyph below the headline block, above the tagline if used.

**Body slides:** White background. Purple (`#7C3AED`) section rule at top. Ink or Black headlines. No bullet lists longer than 4 items. Green (`#3E9B5F`) reserved for deliberate positive signals within tables — not a general-purpose second accent.

## What This Is Not

- **Not a crypto product.** The blockchain anchoring is a record-integrity feature, not a cryptocurrency or token product. Correct framing: "append-only record anchored to the Base blockchain for tamper-evidence" — never denied outright, never made the headline claim.
- **Not a replacement.** Every sales document explicitly states that Alex makes the humans more effective — it does not eliminate roles.
- **Not vaporware.** Every claim must be backed by either a live deployment, an active pilot, or a specific real-world reference. No hypotheticals presented as capabilities.
