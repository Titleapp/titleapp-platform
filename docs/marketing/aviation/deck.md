## Slide 2 — The Problem: 2021

**Title:** A System Went Down. Years of Records Disappeared.

**Visual:** Split screen — left side: a physical pilot logbook on a desk; right side: a broken/loading screen icon with a red X. The contrast is the story.

**Bullets:**
- In 2021, a leading aviation platform failed. Backups were incomplete. Pilots lost years of flight records with no legal recourse.
- Paper logbooks are still the FAA-recognized master record. When the digital system is the only copy, failure is total.
- When a pilot changes operators, their training history becomes the prior employer's property — accessible only by fax request that federal law requires but no one enforces.
- This is not a software gap. It is a records infrastructure gap. And it has been accepted as normal.

## Slide 3 — Current State: The 6-App Stack

**Title:** This Is What "Dispatch" Looks Like Today

**Visual:** Six open browser tabs or app windows shown simultaneously — ForeFlight, RAMCO, a spreadsheet, a physical logbook photo, an email thread, and Protean. The screen is full. Nothing is connected.

**Bullets:**
- A typical Part 135 release decision requires 6 separate tools: navigation, MX status, crew scheduling, pilot currency (paper), FRAT scoring, and the dispatch platform itself.
- None of them share data. The dispatcher assembles the compliance picture manually before every flight.
- After the flight: 15 minutes re-entering data the CAD system already captured.
- If an FAA inspector asks about a release decision from 14 months ago: find the binder, hope it's complete, pray nothing was misfiled.

## Slide 9 — The Long Game

**Title:** The Moat Is the Record Chain.

**Visual:** A timeline stretching from 2026 to 2035. Three milestones marked: (1) "Part 135 operators — records live on the aircraft and the pilot" (2) "Drone delivery: same rules engine, no pilot required" (3) "Portable pilot record: PRIA becomes a verification step."

**Bullets:**
- The drone delivery market is a Part 135 compliance problem at scale — automated, high-volume, no pilot in the aircraft; the same rules engine that dispatches a medevac dispatches a drone
- The portable pilot record makes PRIA obsolete: when the record follows the pilot, prior employers can't lose it, withhold it, or go out of business with it
- Incumbents (RAMCO, CAMP, Protean) are single-employer systems — architecturally incapable of building a portable record chain
- The record chain compounds: every flight, every sign-off, every employer change deepens the moat
